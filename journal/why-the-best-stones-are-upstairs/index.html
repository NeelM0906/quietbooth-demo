Why the best stones are never in the window

On 47th Street the windows are at street level and the serious stones are several floors above. There is a reason for that.

By Miriam Adler, founder, high jewelry

Walk down West 47th Street and you will see a great deal of light in the windows. Much of it is lovely. Very little of it is what a careful buyer is looking for, and the dealers know this.

Light is the problem

Shop windows are lit to flatter every stone equally. Under strong spot lighting a slightly yellow diamond looks white and a poorly cut one looks lively. In daylight, near a north-facing window, the differences come back.

That is why we show stones by appointment, at a table by the window, with a daylight lamp beside it. You see the diamond the way you will see it at dinner and on a grey morning, not only under a jeweller's spotlight.

A diamond should look right in the worst light you will ever wear it in.

Time is the other problem

Choosing a serious stone takes an hour, sometimes two. You should be able to compare three stones side by side, read their reports, and ask the same question twice without feeling rushed. A counter at street level is not built for that.

So we keep the door closed and the room quiet. It is not about exclusivity. It is about giving a decision of this size the conditions it deserves.
